LIVE LESSONS: ENHANCEMENT, PRESENTATION, AND INSIGHTS PROPOSAL
Date: 2026-10-17
Status: Product thinking only. No code changes are included in this proposal.

======================================================================
1. PRIMARY GOAL: LESS TEACHER WORK, BETTER STUDENT INSIGHT
======================================================================

The primary purpose of Live Lessons is to reduce teachers' workload while
making student performance easier to see and act on. The feature should do
the repetitive collecting, organizing, calculating, and summarizing of
lesson evidence automatically, so the teacher can spend less time preparing
reports and checking answers one by one, and more time teaching students.

The product should answer two questions quickly:
1. What is each student understanding, and where does that student need
   support?
2. What is the one useful next action for the teacher or student?

Make Live Lessons a low-workload teaching loop:

PLAN -> TEACH -> CHECK UNDERSTANDING -> RESPOND -> FOLLOW UP

The system should automatically capture lesson answers, summarize class and
individual performance, identify patterns worth a teacher's attention, and
make the next step easy. Teachers should not have to manually count answers,
copy results into separate reports, or inspect every response just to find
which students need help. Students should be able to see their own
performance and a clear, encouraging next step. School leaders should be
able to see broad patterns without mistaking participation for learning.

Product principles:
- Automate routine work; ask the teacher only for decisions that require
  professional judgment.
- Make individual student performance visible, specific, and easy to follow
  over time.
- Put the next useful teaching action ahead of charts and raw event data.
- Generate concise summaries and reports from recorded responses without
  requiring teachers to write them manually.
- Keep student-level evidence available to authorized staff, with sensible
  class and school boundaries.
- Separate engagement, attendance, and correctness; they measure different
  things.
- Treat unanswered questions as missing evidence, not automatically wrong.
- Treat tab visibility and presence data as technical signals, not proof of
  attention, intent, or misconduct.
- Make every score explainable by linking it back to the student's answers
  and the question that produced it.
- Preserve teacher control: insights suggest actions; teachers decide what
  to do.

======================================================================
2. WHAT THE FEATURE ALREADY DOES
======================================================================

The existing foundation is substantial:
- Teachers create, preview, edit, reuse, and organize slide-based lessons.
- Lessons can be linked to a class and subject, scheduled, and presented live.
- Teachers can pause, navigate slides, open and close questions, reveal
  results, hide lesson content, use a class timer, and view the session
  roster.
- Students can join live lessons for their enrolled classes, submit one
  answer per question, and see correctness when results are revealed.
- Lessons can be shared between teachers. A recipient can accept a copy or
  decline the invitation.
- Teachers can bulk-select lessons to share or delete and can bulk-accept or
  bulk-decline incoming lesson invitations.
- Session history supports search, filters, reports, replay, and cleanup.
- Session snapshots preserve the lesson and class roster as they were when
  the session started.
- Reports include attendance, participation, accuracy, response counts,
  duration, per-student information, and time away from the lesson page.
- A teacher can review answers and optionally create linked classwork
  Gradebook entries.

The next opportunity is not to add more metrics for their own sake. It is
to do more of the reporting and pattern-finding automatically, then present
each student's performance and the class's learning needs in a way that is
fast to understand and easy to act on.

======================================================================
3. RECOMMENDED EXPERIENCE BY AUDIENCE
======================================================================

A. TEACHER: BEFORE CLASS

Present a lesson library as a teaching workspace rather than just a file
list:
- "Continue teaching": upcoming or waiting sessions that need attention.
- "My lessons": searchable lessons with class, subject, grade, slide count,
  and last used date.
- "Shared with me": incoming invitations and accepted copies.
- "Recently taught": completed sessions with report and replay shortcuts.
- Clear actions: Preview, Edit, Start, Schedule, Share, Mark done, Delete.
- Bulk actions should be visibly grouped after lessons are selected.
- A lesson preview should show its sequence and indicate which slides contain
  questions, examples, media, or teacher notes.
- Before starting, summarize class, subject, roster size, question count,
  and any setup warnings such as an empty roster or a lesson with no
  questions.

Potential later addition:
- A lesson-preparation checklist: objective, key vocabulary, question
  coverage, estimated duration, and accessible media.
- Allow teachers to duplicate a lesson for adaptation while preserving the
  original and its history.
- Add optional lesson tags such as "review", "new concept", "assessment",
  and "intervention".

B. TEACHER: DURING CLASS

Keep the presentation itself focused and uncluttered. Put live class
monitoring in a companion teacher panel or collapsible drawer so monitoring
does not compete with the lesson content.

The companion panel should answer:
- How many students are on the roster?
- How many have joined?
- For the open question, how many have answered and how many are still
  waiting?
- Is the class ready to reveal results?
- Which answer choices are being selected?
- Which students may need a check-in or additional explanation?

Recommended live sequence:
1. Show a neutral participation count while answers are being collected.
2. Let the teacher decide when to close the question.
3. Show the response distribution without exposing individual answers to
   classmates.
4. Reveal the correct answer and explanation.
5. Offer a short teacher action: discuss, model again, ask a follow-up, or
   continue.

Avoid public student ranking or a high-pressure speed leaderboard. Response
latency can help a teacher notice questions that take longer, but should not
be used by itself to label a student or grade their understanding.

C. TEACHER: AFTER CLASS

The report should be produced automatically when a session ends. The
teacher should not need to assemble it from individual student records.
Lead with a compact summary and a recommended next step:
- Attendance: joined students / roster.
- Participation: students who answered at least one question / roster.
- Understanding: correct answers / submitted answers, alongside the number
  of submitted answers.
- Questions needing review: questions with low accuracy or concentrated
  misconceptions.
- Follow-up candidates: students with repeated unanswered questions, low
  accuracy across several sessions, or a notable change from their own
  baseline.
- A short automatically generated class summary, such as "Most students
  understood equivalent fractions; several selected the numerator-only
  distractor on Question 4."
- A compact list of students who need a follow-up, with the answer evidence
  that explains why they are listed.

Include engagement charts in the report and in the class/student progress
views:
- Class engagement chart: show how the class participated in each lesson,
  then allow the teacher to switch to weekly or monthly summaries.
- Individual student engagement chart: show one student's participation
  across lessons, weeks, or months, so the teacher can see consistency and
  change without comparing the student publicly with classmates.
- Student performance chart: show correctness/understanding separately
  from engagement. A student can participate actively while still needing
  help with the material.
- Let the teacher move from a class chart point to the students and answers
  behind it, and from a student chart point to that lesson's report.

Then provide drill-downs:
- Question view: question text, answer-choice distribution, correct answer,
  explanation, and response count.
- Student view: questions answered, chosen answers, correctness, strengths,
  skills/questions to revisit, and change from that student's own previous
  performance.
- Lesson view: slide sequence and where participation or difficulty changed.
- Session view: replay, timeline, attendance changes, pause/resume, and
  question events.

Keep "Create classwork grades" as an explicit review-and-confirm action.
Show the proposed subject, date, marks, maximum marks, and students before
saving. Never silently create grades from a live-session insight.

The default path should be summary first, detail only when the teacher needs
it. A teacher should be able to identify the students and questions needing
attention without opening every answer. Preserve the detailed answer record
for verification, parent communication, or a closer review.

D. SCHOOL LEADER / DEPARTMENT VIEW

Provide a class- and date-filtered overview focused on instructional
coverage:
- Number of live lessons delivered.
- Classes and subjects with recent lesson activity.
- Participation and question-response trends.
- Class-level engagement charts by lesson, week, and month, with filters
  for class, subject, and date range.
- Question-level areas where many classes show difficulty.
- Lesson reuse and sharing activity.
- Classes with little or no live-lesson activity in the selected period.

Do not rank teachers using raw engagement or answer accuracy. Class size,
lesson difficulty, roster completeness, subject, access to devices, and
teaching purpose all affect the numbers. Use the overview to support
conversation and resource planning, not to create a high-stakes scorecard.

E. STUDENT: PERFORMANCE THEY CAN UNDERSTAND

Give each student a private, age-appropriate view of their own performance:
- Which questions they answered correctly.
- Which ideas or question types they should practise again.
- How their engagement and learning performance are changing across
  lessons, weeks, and months.
- One clear next step, such as reviewing an example or trying a follow-up
  question.

Keep the feedback specific to evidence from the lesson. Avoid class
rankings, public comparison, fixed ability labels, and unexplained scores.
Show missing answers as "not answered" rather than automatically treating
them as incorrect. The teacher controls whether and when a session summary
is shared with students.

======================================================================
4. USEFUL INSIGHTS WE CAN PROVIDE
======================================================================

INSIGHT 1: CLASS PARTICIPATION
Question: Did students have an opportunity to take part?
Evidence: Roster snapshot, joined students, and students submitting answers.
Presentation: Show roster, joined count, and answer count separately.
Action: Check access or invite students who have not joined; check whether
the lesson offered enough answer opportunities.
Caution: Joining is not the same as attending to or understanding a lesson.

INSIGHT 2: ENGAGEMENT OVER TIME
Question: Is each student taking part consistently, and is class
participation changing?
Evidence: Whether each rostered student joined and submitted answers to
available questions in each session.
Presentation: Provide a class chart and a selectable individual-student
chart. Support three views: per lesson/session, weekly, and monthly. Show
the numerator and denominator on every point, such as "8 of 10 students
answered" or "this student answered 3 of 4 available questions."
Action: Identify lessons or periods with participation changes, check
whether students had access, and decide whether a follow-up or class
conversation is needed.
Caution: Engagement is an observable participation measure, not attention,
effort, motivation, or proof of learning. Do not interpret missing sessions
as zero engagement if a student was not on the roster or the lesson was not
available to them.

INSIGHT 3: QUESTION-LEVEL MISCONCEPTIONS
Question: Which concept or distractor caused difficulty?
Evidence: Correctness and selected option per response, linked to the
question and slide.
Presentation: Sort questions by low accuracy, but always show response
count. Identify the most-selected incorrect choice.
Action: Re-teach the concept, explain the distractor, or assign a follow-up
question.
Caution: Small response counts make percentages unstable. Show counts and
avoid drawing strong conclusions from one or two answers.

INSIGHT 4: INDIVIDUAL FOLLOW-UP
Question: Which students may benefit from a teacher check-in?
Evidence: Student's accuracy and participation across multiple sessions,
plus per-question answer history.
Presentation: Provide a private teacher-only list with evidence and a
click-through to the student answer record.
Action: Offer a small-group explanation, alternate example, or one-to-one
check-in.
Caution: Do not label a learner as incapable or disengaged based on one
lesson. Use "may need follow-up" rather than diagnostic language.

INSIGHT 5: CHANGE IN LEARNING OVER TIME
Question: Is understanding improving after instruction or review?
Evidence: Accuracy and response patterns on comparable questions or
repeated lesson objectives across sessions.
Presentation: Provide a separate correctness/performance chart by student
and class, with per-lesson, weekly, and monthly views. Trend across lessons,
weeks, or months only when the questions or objectives are sufficiently
comparable. Keep this chart visually and conceptually separate from
engagement charts.
Action: Decide whether to move on, revisit, or gather more evidence.
Caution: A change in lesson difficulty or question design can make a simple
score comparison misleading.

INSIGHT 6: LESSON EFFECTIVENESS
Question: Which lesson sections invite participation or need refinement?
Evidence: Response coverage and answer-choice patterns by question slide,
compared across uses of the same lesson.
Presentation: Show which questions consistently produce confusion or low
response, with the lesson version and date.
Action: Edit unclear wording, add an example, change distractors, or insert
an additional check for understanding.
Caution: Snapshot each session's lesson version. Later edits must not
rewrite earlier evidence.

INSIGHT 7: RESPONSE TIME
Question: Which questions took longer for this class?
Evidence: Stored answer latency.
Presentation: Median and spread by question, not only an average. Compare
with the same question or lesson when possible.
Action: Review whether instructions were unclear, reading load was high, or
students needed more thinking time.
Caution: Network and device delays affect latency. Do not infer effort,
ability, or distraction from response time alone.

INSIGHT 8: LESSON ADOPTION AND SHARING
Question: Are teachers able to reuse and adapt useful material?
Evidence: Lesson starts, lesson reuse, invitation status, and copied lessons.
Presentation: Show shared lessons, pending invitations, accepted copies,
and recent reuse in the library.
Action: Find useful material to share in a department or subject team.
Caution: Sharing count is not a measure of lesson quality or instructional
impact.

INSIGHT 9: FOLLOW-UP COVERAGE
Question: Was there an instructional response after a low-understanding
signal?
Evidence: Teacher-added follow-up note, later linked activity/session, or
reviewed Gradebook entry (if the product later records this relationship).
Presentation: Mark follow-up as teacher-recorded, not automatically inferred.
Action: Close the teaching loop and see whether the next check improved.
Caution: Do not claim that an intervention caused a result without
appropriate evidence.

======================================================================
5. PRESENTATION AND INFORMATION DESIGN
======================================================================

Use progressive disclosure:

LEVEL 1 - QUICK READ
- A few summary cards with plain-language labels.
- Clear counts, percentages, and denominators.
- One or two evidence-based suggested actions.
- Visible empty states such as "No questions were answered in this session."
- A small class engagement trend chart with a toggle for per lesson, week,
  and month.
- A student-by-student performance table that opens a private individual
  engagement and learning chart.

LEVEL 2 - INVESTIGATE
- Question distributions.
- Student answer table.
- Lesson and session comparisons.
- Filters for date range, class, subject, lesson, and student.
- Interactive class and individual charts. Selecting a point opens its
  lesson/session details and the students or answers included in that point.

LEVEL 3 - AUDIT / EXPORT
- Full answer-level detail.
- Replay timeline and event details.
- Printable class and individual reports.
- Gradebook preview and explicit confirmation.

Visual rules:
- Always pair percentages with counts, e.g. "6 of 10 answered" or
  "4/7 correct"; never show a bare percentage when the denominator matters.
- Use consistent terms: attendance = joined, participation = answered at
  least one question, accuracy = correct among submitted answers.
- Use colour as a secondary signal and include text labels for accessibility.
- Make no-data, incomplete-data, and zero-performance states visibly
  different.
- Let teachers move from any aggregate number to the responses behind it.
- Keep engagement and learning performance as separate chart series or
  panels; do not blend participation and correctness into one score.
- Use lesson/session as the most detailed time point, with week and month
  rollups. Label each time period and show the number of lessons included.
- For weekly/monthly rollups, calculate from total eligible students and
  total available answer opportunities, rather than averaging lesson
  percentages equally. Show the underlying counts so teachers can interpret
  small or uneven periods.
- Preserve the session roster as the denominator for each lesson. For
  trends, distinguish "not rostered / no lesson" from "rostered but did not
  join" and from "joined but did not answer."
- Make each individual chart private to the student and authorized staff;
  do not show student ranking or names on a class-wide chart.
- Provide a print-friendly view that removes controls but preserves context,
  filters, date range, and definitions.

Suggested class report order:
1. Class, teacher, date range, and number of sessions.
2. Class engagement chart with per-lesson, weekly, and monthly views.
3. Attendance and participation summary.
4. Understanding summary with submitted-response denominator and a
   separate performance trend.
5. Questions to revisit.
6. Student follow-up list, with links to individual performance charts.
7. Session-by-session trend and detailed question/answer tables.
8. Method notes and metric definitions.

======================================================================
6. PRACTICAL USE CASES
======================================================================

TEACHER PREPARING TOMORROW'S LESSON
- Review the last lesson's difficult questions.
- Add a worked example or rewrite a confusing prompt.
- Select a short retrieval question to check whether the misconception has
  changed.

TEACHER RESPONDING DURING A LESSON
- Notice that most submitted answers share the same incorrect option.
- Pause the slide sequence, explain why that choice is tempting but wrong,
  and give another example before proceeding.

TEACHER PLANNING SMALL-GROUP SUPPORT
- Use evidence from multiple sessions to identify students who may need
  another explanation.
- Form a temporary group for instruction, not a permanent ability label.
- Recheck the skill in a later lesson.

SUBJECT TEAM SHARING
- Share a lesson with colleagues.
- Recipients accept an independent copy, adapt it for their class, and
  compare their own class evidence without changing the original author's
  lesson or session history.

DEPARTMENT REVIEW
- Look for common topics where several classes show the same misconception.
- Share a strong explanation or example across the department.
- Identify classes needing devices, access support, or planning time before
  interpreting low participation as an instructional issue.

PARENT / STUDENT COMMUNICATION
- If a school later enables student-facing summaries, show specific
  strengths, one practice target, and a next step.
- Do not expose class-level comparisons, other students' answers, or
  teacher-only notes.

7. RECOMMENDED ROADMAP
======================================================================

PHASE 1 - MAKE CURRENT REPORTS ACTIONABLE
Priority: High; low conceptual risk.
- Automatically prepare a concise class summary after every completed
  session; do not make the teacher build it manually.
- Add an at-a-glance student performance table with correct/submitted
  answers, questions missed, and links directly to the supporting answers.
- Automatically group students by the follow-up they may need, such as
  "review Question 4" or "ready for the next concept"; the teacher can
  adjust the groups rather than create them from scratch.
- Surface low-understanding questions and common incorrect choices.
- Add "review this question" links from the summary to the exact slide and
  responses.
- Put each student's strengths and practice targets in a private,
  easy-to-scan view.
- Clarify metric definitions and show counts beside percentages.
- Improve empty, incomplete, and low-sample states so teachers are not
  asked to investigate misleading signals.
- Allow optional teacher follow-up notes, but do not require notes for the
  system to produce useful reports.

PHASE 2 - CLASS AND LESSON TRENDS
Priority: High; requires careful aggregation.
- Build a student performance timeline across lessons and subjects, using
  comparable question sets or clear cautions where questions differ.
- Automatically identify students whose performance is improving, steady,
  or may need another check, based on enough evidence.
- Improve the existing class/date performance report with question-level
  and lesson-level comparisons.
- Reuse prior lesson evidence to help teachers decide what to review next.
- Generate filtered summaries and accessible reports without requiring
  manual copying or spreadsheet work.

PHASE 3 - LIVE TEACHER COACHING PANEL
Priority: Medium; changes the live-teaching UI.
- Add a companion roster and question-response panel.
- Show who has joined and who has answered without interrupting
  presentation.
- Show response distributions only when the teacher closes the question.
- Add a one-click teacher action to revisit a slide or ask a follow-up.
- Do not reveal student-specific correctness publicly.

PHASE 4 - SCHOOL-WIDE INSIGHT OVERVIEW
Priority: Medium; requires access and governance decisions.
- Add class/subject/period filters and department-level aggregation.
- Present coverage and trends, not teacher rankings.
- Establish role-based access, audit logging, minimum group-size rules, and
  a documented purpose for every report.
- Confirm retention, privacy, and school policy for event-level presence
  data.

PHASE 5 - STUDENT PERFORMANCE VIEW
Priority: Later; keep student visibility teacher-controlled.
- Provide a student-friendly view of the student's own performance after a
  lesson when the teacher or school enables it.
- Highlight one demonstrated strength and one next practice opportunity.
- Keep feedback specific to evidence and avoid fixed labels.

======================================================================
8. SUCCESS MEASURES
======================================================================

Measure whether the feature improves teaching workflow and follow-up:
- Teacher time spent preparing, reviewing, and sharing a lesson report
  compared with the current workflow.
- Teacher-reported reduction in manual answer checking and report writing.
- Time from session end to finding students who need follow-up.
- Share of students who can access and understand their own performance
  feedback.
- Share of students with a clear, evidence-based next step after a lesson.
- Share of completed sessions with at least one answerable question.
- Student join and answer coverage, reported separately.
- Share of reports opened after a live session.
- Share of low-understanding reports followed by a teacher-recorded action.
- Repeat performance on comparable questions or targeted follow-up checks.
- Teacher-reported usefulness and time needed to find the next action.
- Lesson reuse and adaptation across a subject team.
- Rate of successful Gradebook preview-to-confirm flows, where used.

Do not define success as maximizing answer accuracy, session length, tab
visibility, or number of lessons created. Those figures can be gamed and do
not necessarily mean learning improved.

======================================================================
9. DATA, PRIVACY, AND INTERPRETATION GUARDRAILS
======================================================================

- Keep answer keys and explanations teacher-only until the teacher reveals
  results, consistent with the current live-session rules.
- Enforce school, class, teacher, and department access on every new
  aggregate and drill-down endpoint.
- Show only the minimum student information required for the teaching task.
- Establish appropriate retention for event timelines and presence data.
- Avoid using focus-loss/presence events for automated discipline,
  behavioral grades, or claims that a student was not paying attention.
- Avoid automated ability labels, public rankings, or unsupported
  intervention recommendations.
- Make the basis of every insight inspectable: date range, sessions,
  question count, response count, exclusions, and formulas.
- Suppress or qualify metrics based on very small response counts.
- Keep incomplete rosters and missing responses visible as data-quality
  issues, not student failures.
- Make Gradebook entry creation explicit and reviewable; analytics should
  never silently alter an official grade.

======================================================================
10. RECOMMENDED FIRST RELEASE
======================================================================

Start with Phase 1. It uses the current report data to reduce manual work
and show student performance without introducing a new scoring system:

1. Automatically produce the class summary, including attendance,
   participation, accuracy, and response counts.
2. Show a student-by-student performance table without requiring the
   teacher to open each student record.
3. Automatically show each question's response distribution and
   most-selected incorrect choice.
4. Give the teacher an evidence-linked list of students and questions that
   may need follow-up, with suggested groups the teacher can adjust.
5. Let the teacher open underlying answers, revisit the lesson, or confirm
   a classwork grade only when needed.
6. Explain how every metric is calculated and identify small samples.

After teachers validate this workflow, build the live companion panel and
then student/class trends. This order first saves teachers time and makes
individual performance visible; broader analytics come after the core
teaching workflow is useful and trusted.
